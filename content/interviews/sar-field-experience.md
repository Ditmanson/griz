# Purpose
Second half of the first podcast-style interview: the same search and rescue handler, focused on what they've seen over the years and on missions. Checklist to work from, not a script; check items off as covered.

## Missions & Career
- [ ] What's the most memorable mission you've worked, and what made it stick with you?
- [ ] What's a moment where the dog did something that genuinely surprised you?
- [ ] What's the hardest thing about this job that people outside of it don't understand?
- [ ] How has your approach to training and handling changed from your first dog to now?
- [ ] What's something you believed early on that field experience later proved wrong?

## Forcefree vs. Traditional vs. Balanced, in Hindsight
- [ ] Has real mission experience shifted where you land on forcefree vs. traditional vs. balanced, compared to how you trained early on?
- [ ] Was there a specific incident in the field that changed your mind about a method or tool?
- [ ] Do you think the pressure of real search work pushes handlers toward more traditional/balanced methods than the broader pet-dog training world favors, or not?

## Classical Conditioning in the Field
- [ ] Can you think of a real mission moment where you saw classical conditioning clearly at work — the dog's response to a cue, sound, or environment?
- [ ] Has a dog ever picked up an unintended association in the field (to a smell, sound, or location) that affected performance?
- [ ] How do you maintain or recondition an association that's degraded after time away from training or a bad experience in the field?

## Operant Conditioning in the Field
- [ ] What's an example of reinforcement history paying off (or failing) during an actual search?
- [ ] Have you seen a dog's behavior fall apart under real-world pressure in a way training didn't predict, and what did you do about it?
- [ ] How do you reinforce in the field when your usual rewards/setup aren't available?
- [ ] Any story about a correction or consequence in the field that taught you something about the dog — or about yourself as a handler?
