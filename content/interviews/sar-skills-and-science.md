# Purpose
First podcast-style interview: a search and rescue dog handler, focused on the craft — reading the dog and training it for the job. Checklist to work from, not a script; check items off as covered.

## Reading the Dog
- [ ] How do you tell the difference between a dog that's genuinely alerting and one that's just excited or distracted?
- [ ] What early signs of stress or shutdown do you watch for in a working dog, and how do you respond in the moment?
- [ ] How do you read drive state (prey, hunt, play) to know when a dog is ready to work vs. needs a break?
- [ ] What does a false alert look like in the dog's body language, and how do you distinguish it from a true find?
- [ ] How much of "reading the dog" is breed/line-specific vs. learned about that individual dog?

## Training for the Job vs. General Obedience
- [ ] Where does SAR-specific training diverge from foundational obedience, and at what stage of the dog's development?
- [ ] How do you train independent decision-making (working out of sight/off-lead) without losing control?
- [ ] What does proofing look like for a SAR skill — generalizing across terrain, scent conditions, and distractions?
- [ ] How do you handle a dog that's technically obedient but doesn't have the drive or nerve for search work?
- [ ] What's a habit from pet-dog obedience that actively works against you in SAR training?

## Forcefree vs. Traditional vs. Balanced
- [ ] Where would you place your own approach on the forcefree-traditional-balanced spectrum, and has that shifted over your career?
- [ ] Are there parts of SAR training where a purely forcefree approach falls short, or where aversives are unnecessary?
- [ ] How do you weigh fallout risk (suppressed signaling, avoidance) when corrections are used in high-stakes search work?
- [ ] Has the working-dog community's stance on these methods shifted during your time doing this, and in which direction?

## Classical Conditioning in Training
- [ ] How do you use classical conditioning to build a dog's alert/indication as an involuntary response rather than a "trick"?
- [ ] What's your process for pairing a target odor with reinforcement so it becomes intrinsically motivating?
- [ ] Have you seen classical conditioning cause problems — a dog conditioned to something you didn't intend (handler tension, equipment, location)?

## Operant Conditioning in Training
- [ ] Which quadrant do you rely on most for search-specific behaviors, and why?
- [ ] How do you use shaping, luring, or capturing to build a search or alert behavior from scratch?
- [ ] How do you decide when a behavior needs to move to a variable reinforcement schedule for field reliability?
- [ ] Any examples of operant conditioning going wrong — extinction bursts, superstitious behavior, resistance you had to work through?
